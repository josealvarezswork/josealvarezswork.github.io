Cuak — Your emotional financial companion

01 — The problem

Most people feel their credit is fine — until suddenly it isn't

Bank dashboards show numbers — balance, limit, due date — but never answer the real question: am I okay? That uncertainty creates anxiety instead of action. Cuak makes the warning visible before it becomes a crisis.

The result: silent distress, reactive behavior (paying only the minimum out of fear) and total disconnection from the financial product.

The problem

Banking apps are technically correct but emotionally cold. Users know how much they owe; whether that puts them in a good or bad position stays unanswered, and the uncertainty paralyzes them.

The opportunity

Design an emotional layer on top of financial data. A mascot that carries the warning in its face — a visible, immediate state the user reads in seconds.

A mascot that reacts to your financial behavior

Cuak connects to credit card data via Open Banking (Belvo) and maps user behavior to one of four emotional states. The mascot's state is the primary interface: it occupies the top of the screen and drives everything below it.

Three tabs. Two of them are designed and working:

The three tabs

Home. Current mascot state + billing cycle summary. Users understand their situation in under 3 seconds.

Progress. Financial behavior history, rendered as trends the user can read at a glance.

More. Settings, account connection (manual or Belvo), alerts and notification preferences.

The card deck

Installment calculator — implemented. Opens from the front card. The user enters an amount and number of installments with a vertical sliding selector, and it calculates in real time: monthly payment, total paid, total interest and amortization table. It uses the card's real rate or estimates from usage level, and blocks the calculation if the amount exceeds available credit.

Gestures on the UI thread. react-native-gesture-handler + Reanimated. Swiping down changes the front card and the calculator reveals below the deck without interrupting the gesture — animations run off the JS thread, so no frames drop.

The principle underneath all three: the emotional state carries the warning. If Cuak is worried, the user feels it before reading a single number.

Four states. One clear signal.

The system maps the user's financial behavior to four emotional states. Each state has a visual expression, a color and a suggested action — reducing the cognitive friction of interpreting financial data.

Cuak advises you before you spend

Banking apps tell you what you already spent. Cuak shows you what you should do before making the decision. The Purchase Simulator is the product's flagship feature.

The user enters the amount of a future purchase — a TV, a trip. The backend engine (cycle_service.py) analyzes the billing cycle and responds with the optimal strategy.

Buy today, or wait one day

If you buy today 21 days

Interest-free. Cuak is happy — enough time to pay at no cost.

If you buy tomorrow 50 days

Interest-free, but a full new cycle. Cuak warns you first.

Cuak answers before the decision, not after it. Buying today lands inside the current cycle; one day past the billing date opens a whole new one.

The behavioral loop

If the user makes the smart decision, Cuak rewards them instantly. If they try to overdraw, Cuak switches to Alert state before the mistake happens, so the limit arrives as a face they recognize rather than a figure they have to interpret.

05 — The Retention Engine

From Apprentice to Credit Master

Good financial decisions generate Experience Points (XP). This positive reinforcement turns debt payment from a stressful chore into a rewarding experience — the same mechanism that gives Duolingo 40M daily active users.

Behavioral Scoring and B2B Lead Generation

Cuak flips the traditional banking model. Instead of charging users for their financial mistakes, Cuak charges banks for their best customers.

B2C — Freemium $9.99 / month

Premium plan with unlimited simulations, multi-card analysis and advanced proactive alerts. The free plan includes 1 card and 3 simulations per month.

B2B — The real business

By tracking the user's daily discipline through gamification, Cuak builds a Behavioral Score. A "Credit Master" level user with a consistently happy Cuak is the zero-risk customer banks actively look for.

CPA model: Banks pay an acquisition fee for every behaviorally pre-qualified lead. The internal AI recommends higher-tier financial products at the exact moment of user maturity.

09 — What's next

From MVP to Production Integration

The emotional state system is designed and the simulator's core flow works in isolation. The next strategic steps are:

  1. Mock data to Open Banking

    Finish sandbox testing and connect the Belvo API in production — real-time ingestion and sync of the user's transactions.

  2. Testing with real users

    Measure whether the emotional signal actually curbs impulsive purchases against real banking data, not mocks.

  3. Deploying the emotional scoring

    Turn validated behavior into a B2B data layer banks and financial institutions can buy.

Further out: a sponsored rewards marketplace. The XP economy becomes tangible — Credit Master users redeem points with partner banks and merchants for cinema tickets, restaurants, in-store discounts. The difference from a traditional points programme is what earns them: disciplined financial behavior, not spending volume. The partner reaches a user with verified credit health at minimal acquisition cost.

The visual system behind Cuak

Every emotional state, color and component is codified into a reusable system — palette built for emotional clarity, the 60/40 zoning model, and a full UI kit.

Before it reports anything

These are the only two screens where Cuak appears with no number attached — no balance, no state colour, no verdict. The order is deliberate: the user has to read the duck as a companion before it starts telling them things they would rather not hear.

One primary action, four screens. The purple behind Continue is #6C43DA — the same value that carries Wait and earn +50 XP in the simulator and the add button on the card deck. Wordmark, cream surface and type scale carry over untouched. The first screen is already the system, not a splash built for it.
