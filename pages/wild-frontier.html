01 · Design approach

Four rules the whole system answers to.

Clarity first

Every screen answers "what needs me right now" before it answers anything else. Resource state and unit status sit above the fold, always.

Tactical feel

The hex grid and terrain-aware iconography come from wargaming, not mobile-game convention — the map reads like a plan, not a postcard.

Immersive world

Terrain sits behind a translucent HUD plate instead of a flat panel, so the jungle stays present without ever competing with a number.

Built for mobile

Every primary action lives inside one thumb's reach at the bottom edge. Nothing critical sits in the corners.

04 · Process

The terrain went through two passes.

The first version used a full-opacity painted jungle behind the HUD. In testing it looked great and read terribly — resource numbers took over two seconds to parse against the busy background. The terrain moved behind a translucent dark plate and dropped in saturation; parse time is the reason every number on this page sits on its own panel instead of directly on the map.

"If the number and the number's plate aren't the same shape everywhere, the player re-learns the HUD every screen."

05 · Key takeaways

What this project is actually about.

Readability under a translucent plate beats a beautiful background every time a number has to be trusted mid-decision.

Color-coding resources to a fixed palette does more for scannability than any icon redesign could.

A status badge (Ready / Moving / Fortified / Wounded) carries more decision-relevant information than a health bar alone.
